Origin

A humble man in Norway is the owner of a very unique Porsche 911.

The car rolled off the assembly line as a 991 Turbo S, but after a 12-month transformation that included custom work by Porsche, it’s been unofficially reimagined as a ‘Turbo RS’ with 880hp (and counting) to boot.

Body

At a quick glance the car looks like a GT2 RS.

But look closer and you start to see that all is not as it first seems. The front bumper, hood and side skirts are all GT2 RS parts, but they’ve been modified to fit the Turbo S model perfectly, with fully functional air intakes and outlets at the front. It wasn’t an easy job, but Porsche made it work.

The wing

Around the back, the owner opted not for the GT2 RS wing, but a slightly less elevated 991.2 GT3 RS item.

Wheels

The original plan was to use the GT2 RS wheels.

After Porsche had weaved their magic, the car was brought back from Germany and left in the capable hands of a Norwegian legend, Espen Olsen, who took it to a whole new level in the performance department.

But there was an issue that ultimately meant the wheel idea was scrapped. This is where Vossen stepped in and built a one-off set of GNS-1 wheels – 21×9.5-inch front and 21×12.5-inch rear – finished in matte gunmetal.

Power

Lift the rear deck lid and you’ll find the same twin-turbocharged 3.8-liter flat six found in the GT2 RS, albeit with modified intakes and turbos to bring around 880hp and 1050Nm to the party.

Purpose

You can’t fit your kids in the back when you’ve only got two seats.

You might be wondering why the owner didn’t save himself a whole lot of trouble and just buy a GT2 RS to begin with, but the answer to that is simple. This custom creation solves that issue, while the AWD makes it Norway-friendly for wintertime use.

It’s also a pretty nice addition to a collection that includes an Audi RS2 and a Ford Escort RS Cosworth.

One of one

A serial-numbered plaque, engraved not printed. The same name set into the carbon sill. Even the dash was reprogrammed to script ‘turbo RS’ on every start-up — proof, three times over, that this car will never exist again.
